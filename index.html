Lotion math

The recipe is four percentages and a prayer, the jar count is a guess, and the preservative line reads "a splash". Lotion math splits the batch into exact grams, turns grams into jars with an honest density, and checks the preservative against a labeled band. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Batch split - batch size and phase percentages become exact grams, with the 100% check.
  • Jar plan - batch grams become milliliters and labeled jars with the spare.
  • Preservative check - grams in the batch become a percent checked against the labeled band.

Labeled norms

Labeled starting phases for a basic lotion: about 70% water, 24% oils, 5% emulsifier, 1% preservative. Labeled density: about 0.95 g per ml. Labeled jar size: 100 ml. Labeled preservative band: 0.5-1.5% of the batch. These are reference values, not rules - your preservative's own instructions are the authority on its dose, and water-based products grow microbes without one.
